Its `designMd` is strong and worth mining even if the visual direction isn't taken wholesale. Notable rules:
- **No 1px borders.** Define boundaries with background shifts and tonal layering, not outlines. If a border is unavoidable (input fields), use `outline-variant` at 15–20% opacity.
- **Tonal depth over drop shadows.** When lift is needed: tinted shadow `rgba(45,41,38,0.06)`, 32–64px blur.
- **Non-standard spacing base** — 0.35rem, deliberately not a 4px or 8px system.
- Never pure black; use `#2D2926`. Never 0px corners. Avoid generic success-green — signal completion with brand teal or terracotta.

## 5. Competitive intelligence

### Shopify Shop app — direct competitor, order-tracking side

Detection is **keyword-based email scanning**, not ML: after connecting Gmail or Outlook, Shop scans the **last 30 days** for phrases like *"tracking number"* and *"track your package"*.

Three ingestion tiers:

1. **Shop Pay / Shopify-powered stores** — order appears automatically, first-party, no email involved. Appears the moment the merchant generates a shipping label.
2. **Connected Gmail / Outlook** — the keyword scan above.
3. **Manual add** — the documented fallback, because *"Shop can't read some tracking emails properly."*

Control surface: Account → Connections → Disconnect. The primary sign-in email cannot be removed this way.

> **The strategic trap.** Tier 1 is *why* tier 2 works for Shop — Shopify is already merchant-of-record for a large share of those orders, so its best data is free and structured, and the email scan only covers the long tail.
>
> Return-It has no tier 1 equivalent. Copying only the email-scan half means paying the **full compliance cost for the lowest-quality signal**. Mimic the *feel* (instant recognition, zero data entry, a live status object per order); do not assume the *architecture* transfers.

### Findings that challenge the plan (2026-08-26)

Full detail in `docs/competitive-teardown.md`. The three that matter:

1. **Royal Mail Parcel Collect is 30p and brings a pre-printed label** — it competes directly with the proposed £1.99 carrier-direct tier and has already solved the printer objection. That tier needs an answer to *"why not just use Royal Mail?"*
2. **Every consumer-paid returns-pickup company has died or pivoted to B2B** — Shyp, Doorman, Doddle's consumer arm, Returnmates (raised $25.6M, discontinued consumer pickup, kept the B2B operation). Stated causes are frequency and pricing, not operations. Returns happen ~4×/year.
3. **Orderly already ships this entire front end free** — email ingestion, deadline countdowns, home pickup — and reached ~5,000 users in 2.5 years.

**And the reframe:** the unowned white space is the **pre-label gap**. Royal Mail can't help until you hold a barcoded label; the ~45 minutes before that — finding the order, opening the portal, generating the return, getting the label — belongs to nobody in the UK. That makes the parser the product, not the plumbing.

**Decision (2026-08-26):** hold the plan and let the pilot test pricing directly, rather than treating the US graveyard as a verdict. Requires willingness-to-pay instrumentation designed in from the start.

### How fulfilment actually works

Full detail in `docs/carrier-integration-research.md`.

**No UK carrier has a self-serve API for collecting a third party's pre-paid label.** But **point-to-point couriers (Stuart, Gophr) never look at the label** — so any courier can move the parcel to whatever drop-off point the label implies. The constraint is economics, not access, and **batching is the product**.

✅ **Royal Mail permits agent booking.** T&Cs downloaded manually and read (`docs/reference/`). **Clause 14.7:** *"you can use another person to carry out any of your duties (as your agent or otherwise) as long as you notify us first."* Three conditions: notify RM first, the user stays liable for our actions as their agent, and we must comply with the agreement including the prohibited-goods list. No reseller prohibition anywhere in the document.

⚠️ Remaining risks: **Amazon suspended this exact model on Royal Mail's rail on 13 Aug 2026**; and clause 2.2 scopes that document to postage *bought through Click & Drop*, so the governing terms for a retailer's pre-paid label may differ — confirm when notifying RM under 14.7.

### Other players

| Competitor | What they do | The gap Return-It fills |
|---|---|---|
| Royal Mail Parcel Collect | Cheap (~30p) doorstep collection | Royal Mail labels only. No cross-carrier, no aggregation, no printing. |
| Collect+ | 14k drop-off points, in-store label printing | You still travel. Drop-off, not pickup. |
| Evri | ParcelShop, lockers, doorstep collection | Carrier-specific. Not return-label-first. |
| Parcel2Go | Shipping broker, compare carriers | "Create a shipment" flow. Not built for a label you already have. |
| Orderly | Email-connected order tracking + return alerts | Tracks but never collects. No fulfilment layer. |

**Return-It's edge:** aggregation **plus** universal fulfilment (pickup + printing + drop-off injection). The only service where *"I already have a label"* is the starting point.

## 6. Hard constraints

### Google OAuth — the one that shapes the architecture

`gmail.readonly` is a **restricted** scope. This is not a formality:

- Apps using restricted scopes need a **CASA security assessment** by a Google-approved lab before going public, with **annual re-certification**. Reported costs range widely — roughly **$500 to $75k** depending on tier and assessor.
- Until verified, the OAuth consent screen sits in **Testing** status, which caps you at **100 manually-allowlisted test users**.
- ⚠️ **In Testing status, refresh tokens expire after 7 days.** Any use after that returns `invalid_grant`.

That last point is the killer for a *background scanning* product: every pilot user would have to re-authorise weekly. It doesn't block a pilot, but it means Gmail OAuth cannot be the only ingestion path at pilot scale.

Sources: [Google restricted scope verification](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/production-readiness/restricted-scope-verification) · [7-day refresh token expiry](https://www.unipile.com/google-oauth-refresh-token/) · [100 test-user cap](https://www.unipile.com/google-oauth-100-user-limit/)

### GDPR / privacy (from the PRD, line 376+)

Email scanning must be **explicit opt-in**, **transparent** ("we only scan for return confirmations"), and **revocable at any time**. Labels and pickup details encrypted at rest, served via signed URLs, strict access control, audit logs on all admin actions.

### Unit economics (concierge mode)

Density is the whole business. At £10.99:

| Pickups/hour | Cost/pickup | Margin |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | ~£12.61 | **Loss** |
| 3 | ~£9.21 | ~£1.78 (16%) |
| 4 | ~£7.51 | ~£3.48 (32%) |
| 5 | ~£6.49 | ~£4.50 (41%) |

Product and ops must *manufacture* density: batching incentives, time-window pricing, geographic rollout starting with the densest London postcodes.
